# Birds

The birds collection is one of the most valuable scientific resources in the institution, bringing together specimens, records, and associated data that allow researchers to study avian diversity, evolution, distribution, and conservation over time.

## Overview

This collection includes preserved bird specimens from multiple regions and ecosystems, with particular relevance for the study of Colombian biodiversity. It supports work in taxonomy, systematics, ecology, biogeography, and environmental change.

## What the collection contains

The collection may include:

- Study skins and mounted specimens
- Skeletons and anatomical preparations
- Nests, eggs, and other reproductive materials
- Tissue samples and genetic material
- Field notes, labels, and associated metadata

Each specimen is part of a broader record that links morphology, geography, date, collector, and habitat information.

## Why it matters

Birds are among the best-studied vertebrate groups, yet many species remain poorly documented in tropical regions. This collection helps fill critical information gaps by providing reference material for:

- Species identification
- Taxonomic revisions
- Population and distribution studies
- Conservation assessments
- Environmental monitoring

## Research and education

The birds collection is used by researchers, students, and institutions to compare specimens, verify identifications, and support scientific publications. It also plays an important role in education by helping visitors and students understand biodiversity, evolution, and the importance of natural history collections.

## Curatorial value

Careful curation ensures that specimens remain preserved, documented, and accessible for future generations. Digital records and standardized metadata improve discoverability and strengthen the link between museum collections and contemporary research.

## Access and stewardship

The collection is managed as a public scientific resource, with procedures in place to ensure responsible use, proper preservation, and ethical handling of specimens. Its long-term value depends on continued documentation, conservation, and collaboration with the scientific community.

Birds are not only indicators of environmental health, but also essential witnesses of the biological richness of the planet. This collection helps preserve that legacy for research, education, and conservation.
